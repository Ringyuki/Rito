Vertical text is laid out and painted as Chromium does it. A vertical-rl chapter's boxes take their margins, padding, borders and sizes on the sides its flow puts them, and their backgrounds and borders paint there; each line hangs from its central baseline; each upright glyph stands in its face's vertical forms at the font's vertical origin and advances down the column by its vertical advance (by the face's whole-pixel height in a face without vertical metrics), and characters that lie sideways in a mixed line (Latin, digits) are set turned a quarter; a ruby annotation reaches into the space before its line and stands on its base where Chromium places it; an image sizes to the column's length; `text-orientation` (with its `-webkit-` and `-epub-` spellings) reaches the engine; and `text-combine-upright: all` sets its text as one horizontal string within an em of the column, in the face's half-, third- or quarter-width forms or scaled when the string is wider than 1.1em. An inline box whose writing mode differs from its parent's lays out as an inline-block across the parent's lines, and a block's minimum and maximum block size (`min-height`/`max-height`, or `min-width`/`max-width` in a vertical flow) bound it. An artifact's font references carry `features` (reader protocol 9): a reference with features is the same bytes as a face, registered under a family of its own with those OpenType features on (`vert`, the vertical forms; `hwid`, `twid` and `qwid`, the narrower forms a combined string takes), which the browser reader registers as its `font-feature-settings` descriptor.
