Page turns separate physics from presentation: the spring, the drag, the commit decision and the rubber band are shared, and an `effect` decides what is drawn — `'slide'` (default), `'cover'` (later pages slide over earlier ones with a shadow, 24 CSS px wide on every display, that fades as its sheet leaves the frame), `'fade'` (cross-fade) or `'none'` (instant), or a custom `TransitionEffect` returning the layers to draw. `springPresets` (`default`, `snappy`, `gentle`) tune the spring; `configureTransition` changes either live; while the system asks for reduced motion turns cross-fade unless `respectReducedMotion: false`. `transitionStart` / `transitionEnd` now have one owner: every start is answered by exactly one end, and `transitionEnd` carries an `outcome` (`committed`, `cancelled`, `interrupted`) — a relayout or a jump during a turn ends it as interrupted instead of leaving it open, and a newer turn settles the turn in flight at once. React adds `useReaderTransition(controller)` → `{ isTransitioning, direction }`. How a turn moves is replaceable too: `physics` in the transition options (or on an effect) decides how far a finger carries the turn (`travel`), how the ends of the book resist (`resist`), whether a release turns the page (`lands`) and the motion that carries it to rest (`settle`), which also says when the turn is over. A frame tells an effect what is moving the turn (`phase`), how fast (`velocity`) and where the finger took hold and where it is (`touch`); a scene says where the pages are bound (`spine`), and a page's `version` changes whenever it is painted again.
