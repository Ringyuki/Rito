`createPaperTurn()` turns a page as a leaf of a book. The leaf stays bound at the spine, bends round a cone as it lifts — arched in the air, the corner it is taken by leading — swings over the spine and comes down on the far side edge first, the arch settling after; paper bends without stretching, and the leaf is never folded. Under a finger the point taken hold of stays under it; turned by a key or a call it is off at once and lands softly. It is drawn with WebGL in three dimensions, seen from above the spread, both faces softly lit and its shadow on the pages beneath: with two pages a spread it carries the next page on its back, with one it swings up and out of sight past the left edge. It is an effect that brings its own physics, built on the same `paint`, `physics`, `touch`, `spine` and `version` every application can use, and where WebGL is not to be had it paints the built-in `cover`. In the demo on an Apple M3, a turn by key took 0.39 s at two pages a spread and 0.31 s at one, a frame of it 0.4 ms of script at the median (0.5 ms at the 95th percentile; the first frame of the first turn, which compiles the shaders and uploads the page, 11–30 ms), turns ran at the display's 60 frames a second, and the last frame of a turn matched the page at rest.
