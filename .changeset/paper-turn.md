`createPaperTurn()` turns a page as a leaf of a book. The leaf stays bound at the spine, bends round a cone as it lifts — arched in the air, the corner it is taken by leading — swings over the spine and comes down on the far side edge first, the arch settling after; paper bends without stretching, and the leaf is never folded. Under a finger the point taken hold of stays under it; turned by a key or a call it is off at once and lands softly. It is drawn with WebGL in three dimensions, seen from above the spread, and one light serves the leaf's shading and its shadow: the lamp's shadow is found as percentage-closer soft shadows are, sharp where the leaf meets the page and soft where it stands high above it, the room's light dims where the leaf hides the sky, with the light white pages hand back and forth restored by the published fit for surfaces of one albedo, and a sheet lets through the share of light office paper does. With two pages a spread the leaf carries the next page on its back, with one it swings up and out of sight past the left edge. It is an effect that brings its own physics, built on the same `paint`, `physics`, `touch`, `spine` and `version` every application can use, and where WebGL is not to be had it paints the built-in `cover`. In the demo on an Apple M3, a turn by key took 0.39 s at two pages a spread and 0.31 s at one, a frame of it 0.5 ms of script at the median (0.6–0.9 ms at the 95th percentile; the first frame of the first turn, which compiles the shaders and uploads the page, 13–23 ms), turns ran at the display's 60 frames a second, and the last frame of a turn matched the page at rest.
