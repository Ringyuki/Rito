`createPaperTurn()` turns a page as a sheet of paper. The sheet is simulated every frame as a grid of particles — bound at the spine, unable to stretch, stiff against bending, falling under gravity onto the pages beneath — and carried by the point a finger took hold of, so it bows behind the hand, twists when taken by a corner, and the turn is over when it lies still on the page it lands on. It is drawn with WebGL, both faces lit and a soft shadow under it: with two pages a spread it turns over the spine like a leaf and carries the next page on its back, with one it leaves by the left edge and a turn back brings the last sheet in to meet the finger. It is an effect that brings its own physics, built on the same `paint`, `physics`, `touch`, `spine` and `version` every application can use, and where WebGL is not to be had it paints the built-in `cover`. In the demo on an Apple M3, at one and at two pages a spread, a frame of it took 1.6 ms of script at the median (2.4 ms at the 95th percentile; the first frame of the first turn, which compiles the shaders and uploads the page, 21–27 ms), turns ran at the display's 60 frames a second, and the last frame of a turn matched the page at rest pixel for pixel.
