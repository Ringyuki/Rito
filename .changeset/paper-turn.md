`createPaperTurn()` turns a page as a sheet of paper. The sheet is held at a point — under the finger, or by its lower outer corner when no finger turns it — and rolls back over itself between where that point was and where it is: flat on either side of a round fold, the shape paper takes because it bends without stretching. It is drawn with WebGL in three dimensions, both faces, lit along the roll, with the shadow of the lifted paper on what lies beneath: with two pages a spread it turns over the spine like a leaf and carries the next page on its back, with one it leaves by the left edge and a turn back brings the last sheet in to meet the finger. It is an effect that brings its own physics, built on the same `paint`, `physics`, `touch`, `spine` and `version` every application can use; the turn is over when the paper lies flat again, and where WebGL is not to be had it paints the built-in `cover`. In the demo on an Apple M3, at one and at two pages a spread, a frame of it took 0.3 ms of script at the median (0.5 ms at the 95th percentile; the first frame of the first turn, which compiles the shaders and uploads the page, 15–30 ms), turns ran at the display's 60 frames a second, the picture changed from frame to frame without a jump through a turn, and the last frame of a turn matched the page at rest pixel for pixel.
